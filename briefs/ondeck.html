Executive Summary
OnDeck Australia is a Sydney-based, management-owned online lender of unsecured small business loans. It has funded A$1.2bn to date since launching in 2015. Demand is surging, with applications up 120% year on year in February 2026, a record month. Automation has cut approvals for loans under A$200k from about 6 hours to 12 minutes. Reporting still rests on one person, and the Head of Data and Analytics is based in Dallas. The company is creating a Sydney-based reporting and prioritisation lead to give executives one trusted set of numbers and a clear order of work as funders and ASIC look more closely at non-bank lenders.

Stage 3: Top 5 Experiences to Lead With

A first interview will probably test three things: can you make the numbers trusted, can you decide what matters most and defend it, and can you do it without becoming the bottleneck. These five stories cover those three, plus performance monitoring and executive influence.

1

Trusted KPIs across business areas (Cisco, Data Solutions & Insights Lead)

Why it matters here: The JD asks you to "establish reporting standards, governance" and make reporting "timely, accurate, consistent". It answers the first half of the business problem: one trusted set of numbers (H1).
STeams saw different numbers for the same KPI (1 to 5% gaps), causing 3 to 5 escalations a month. TReduce inconsistency to zero and restore confidence in the numbers. AMoved logic from Power BI into shared SQL single-source views. Added plain-English data catalogues, master dashboards per business area and tripwire alerts for variances over 2%. R27 KPIs from 62% to 100% consistency. Dashboards from 70+ to 46. Escalations from 3 to 5 a month to zero. Workspace health from 60% to 93%.
"The first thing I fix is whether people trust the number. At Cisco the same KPI showed different values depending on which dashboard you opened."
Likely question: "How do you make sure executives trust the reporting?" · Source: Resume (DS&I Lead); STAR "Trusted metrics and KPI Consistency"; portfolio case study 1.
