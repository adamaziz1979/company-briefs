1. Background

↑ Back to Top

Business and trajectory

References: Audinate, Dante platform and product overview | Dante, What is Dante? | Audinate FY23 Results, 21-Aug-2023 | Audinate 1H24 Results, 12-Feb-2024 | Audinate FY24 Results, 19-Aug-2024 | Audinate FY25 Results, 18-Aug-2025 | Audinate 1H26 Results, 16-Feb-2026

Observed fact. Audinate’s Dante platform replaces dedicated AV cabling with standard IP networking and supports audio, video and control. Its product set spans chips, modules, reference designs, embedded software, end-user software, adaptors and network-management products. The OEM model starts with a design win, typically followed by a 12–24 month product-development cycle and then repeat revenue once manufacturing begins.

Observed turning points.

  • FY23 and 1H24 marked the post-pandemic and post-chip-shortage earnings acceleration.
  • FY24 reached US$60.0 million revenue and US$44.5 million gross profit, but the company also warned that shorter lead times and channel inventory rebalancing would reduce FY25 visibility.
  • FY25 revenue subsequently fell to US$40.0 million and underlying EBITDA to A$0.7 million, despite gross margin rising to 82.1% as the mix shifted toward software.
  • In 1H26, revenue returned to 12% growth and gross margin remained above 82%, but underlying EBITDA was negative A$2.3 million and the company implemented organisational changes targeting about A$5 million of annualised cost benefit.

Strategic evolution. Audinate is broadening from a dominant audio-networking technology into an audio, video, control and cloud platform. The transition includes Dante Director, embedded software, subscription products, Dante video and the Iris camera-control acquisition. This creates more valuable recurring-revenue opportunities, but also makes forecasting, product economics, commercial attribution and executive reporting materially more complex.

Hypothesis: why the role now. The role is being created to build a single commercial truth across Finance, Sales, Marketing, Product and Strategy at the point where Audinate’s old demand signals have become less reliable and its product model has become more diverse. The immediate need is not simply better dashboards. It is a governed decision system that connects OEM design wins, channel inventory, product adoption, software usage, revenue, gross profit, investment and cash flow, and gives the ELT and Board earlier warning when assumptions change.

Understanding the Return Measures

Methodology note: The original stock fields are trailing, backward-looking returns. For this event analysis, 1-day, 5-day and 20-day returns were recalculated as forward-looking cumulative returns after each event.

MeasureWhat it tells us
1-Day ReturnImmediate market reaction following an announcement.
5-Day ReturnWhether buying or selling momentum continued after investors assessed the news.
20-Day ReturnWhether the market confirmed or reversed its initial reaction over about one month.
60-Day ReturnWhether positive or negative sentiment persisted over approximately one quarter.
252-Day ReturnLong-term trend and performance over approximately one trading year.
Example: If Audinate announces earnings today, the 1-day return shows the immediate reaction; the 5-day return shows whether buying or selling continued; and the 20-day return shows whether investors reinforced or changed their initial view.

The returns describe what followed the event; they do not prove causation.

3. Why the Findings Matter

Likely mandate of the role

References: Audinate, Senior Manager, Data and Insights job description | Audinate FY25 Results, 18-Aug-2025 | Audinate 1H26 Results, 16-Feb-2026

The evidence points to a mandate centred on commercial visibility and executive confidence:

  • Build a governed platform and common semantic model across Finance, Sales, Product, Marketing and Strategy.
  • Create leading indicators from design wins through OEM development, launch, ordering, deployment and recurring usage.
  • Separate underlying demand from inventory cycles and order timing.
  • Measure product economics across hardware, embedded software, subscriptions, cloud and acquisitions.
  • Improve forecast accuracy, scenario planning and early-warning reporting.
  • Give the ELT and Board a concise, repeatable operating narrative tied to commercial actions.
  • Enable self-service without allowing metric definitions to fragment.

4. Executive Conclusion

↑ Back to Top

References: Audinate, Senior Manager, Data and Insights job description | Audinate FY24 Results, 19-Aug-2024 | Audinate FY25 Results, 18-Aug-2025 | Audinate 1H26 Results, 16-Feb-2026
  1. Audinate retains a strong platform and network-effect position, but investor expectations have changed. Ecosystem growth, OEM design wins and product breadth remain important, yet the share-price evidence shows that guidance credibility, demand visibility and execution now have greater influence on valuation.
  2. The CFO transition created short-term investor uncertainty but is not a convincing explanation for FY25 underperformance. The resignation announcement produced a noticeable near-term share-price response, but the evidence points much more directly to OEM inventory rebalancing, shorter ordering horizons and softer end demand as the causes of the operating decline.
  3. Trade shows appear to support ecosystem adoption, training and OEM engagement, but they are not reliable standalone financial catalysts. Their average immediate market impact was small and mixed. The data does not support the stronger claim that trade-show demand caused the FY25 visibility problem.
  4. The FY25 correction exposed a structural information problem. Management needs to distinguish sell-in from sell-through, channel inventory from end demand, and temporary ordering movements from durable product adoption. This creates a credible need for real-time demand sensing, integrated inventory visibility, scenario planning and AI-enabled forecasting.
  5. Audinate is making a material shift toward software, cloud management and recurring revenue, but it is not becoming a pure SaaS company. The strategic direction resembles Cisco's software transition, although Audinate remains more dependent on the physical Dante-enabled device ecosystem. The likely destination is a high-margin, software-led AV platform built on an essential hardware and OEM foundation.
  6. The business is becoming substantially harder to manage analytically. Audio hardware, embedded software, desktop subscriptions, cloud services, video, control and Iris have different revenue timing, margins, adoption signals and data sources. A common commercial model is therefore becoming a management requirement rather than a reporting enhancement.

Most plausible reason for creating the function: to restore a reliable, cross-functional commercial picture while Audinate moves from a simpler component-led growth model to a multi-product platform business operating with shorter lead times, less dependable order visibility and a growing mix of software and recurring revenue.

Problem to position yourself as solving: create the trusted data foundation and decision cadence that connects trade-show engagement, training, OEM design wins, development pipelines, product launches, channel inventory, actual licence consumption and customer usage to revenue, margin, costs and cash. This would allow leaders to identify changes earlier, improve forecasts and communicate guidance with greater confidence.

Strongest interview question: “How do you currently connect OEM design wins, the 12–24 month development cycle, product launches, manufacturer orders, channel inventory, software licence consumption and end-customer demand, and where does that chain break today?”

1. Did the CFO change have a big impact on the company and its performance?

References: Supplied stock and event datasets | Audinate CFO resignation announcement, 27-May-2024 | Audinate CFO appointment announcement, 14-Oct-2024 | Audinate FY25 Results

Hard evidence

Rob Goss announced his resignation on 27-May-2024, citing personal reasons. The transition was orderly: he remained through the FY24 results, annual report and AGM preparation and supported the handover. Audinate described the company as being left in a strong financial position.

The supplied share-price data shows a noticeable initial reaction to the resignation announcement:

WindowObserved return
1 trading dayapproximately −6.4%
5 trading daysapproximately −8.5%
20 trading daysapproximately −4.0%
Event-day volumeapproximately 2× the 30-day average

This indicates that investors initially regarded the departure as a risk, although a meaningful part of the five-day decline had moderated by the end of the 20-day window.

Chris Rollinson's appointment on 14-Oct-2024 generated almost no immediate reaction: approximately +0.2% after one day. The five-day and 20-day returns were approximately −5.0% and −6.6%, but those windows include the much more important 22-Oct-2024 FY25 guidance downgrade. They cannot reasonably be attributed to the appointment itself.

The incoming CFO's disclosed background was weighted toward commercial finance, governance, transformation, restructuring, M&A and capital raising. Audinate described the appointment as occurring at a pivotal point in the company's evolution.

FY25 revenue subsequently fell from US$60.0 million to US$40.0 million, while underlying EBITDA declined from approximately A$20.3 million to A$0.7 million. Audinate attributed this principally to inventory and industry conditions, including OEM inventory overhang, shorter order lead times, softer end demand and lower hardware orders, rather than to the CFO transition.

Inference / limited speculation

The resignation probably had a short-term effect on investor confidence because Rob Goss had been with Audinate since the IPO. There is no public evidence that it caused the deterioration in revenue or profitability.

It is plausible that Chris Rollinson materially influenced the company's response to the downturn through cost discipline, capital allocation, acquisition execution and improved financial planning. Public reporting does not provide enough evidence to isolate or quantify his individual contribution.

Conclusion: The CFO resignation produced a noticeable but relatively short-lived market reaction. The evidence does not support treating the CFO change as a major cause of FY25 operating underperformance. The operational and demand-visibility problems were substantially more important.

Some individual periods were positive, including approximately +29.7% over 20 days after ISE 2019, +29.2% over 20 days after ISE 2022 and +7.8% on day one after InfoComm 2019. Other shows produced negative or mixed results. Several event windows also overlapped with financial results, interest-rate decisions, acquisitions or broader market movements, preventing reliable causal attribution.

Hard evidence: commercial adoption

Audinate increased FY24 sales and marketing expenditure partly through advertising, rebranding, travel and trade-show activity. During the same year:

  • trained AV professionals increased by 22%;
  • the marketing database increased by 20%;
  • website visitors increased by 22%;
  • OEMs shipping Dante products increased from 400 to 460; and
  • Dante-enabled products increased from 3,853 to 4,176.

These measures support the view that industry engagement, product demonstrations, education and certification contributed to ecosystem adoption. They do not isolate the effect of trade shows from Audinate's wider marketing, training, OEM and product-development activity.

Audinate also states that an OEM design win is generally followed by a 12–24 month development cycle before repeat manufacturing revenue begins. A successful trade show would therefore be expected to affect future product interest, design wins and launches rather than immediately change quarterly revenue.

Hard evidence: FY25 visibility

The evidence does not establish that trade-show-driven demand caused the FY25 visibility problem. Audinate attributed reduced visibility to:

  • shorter customer order lead times;
  • OEM and channel inventory rebalancing;
  • customers consuming excess raw-material and finished-goods inventory; and
  • softer-than-expected end-user demand.

Audinate had previously benefited from unfulfilled orders and elevated hardware demand after component shortages. When product availability normalised, customers no longer needed to place long-dated orders and instead consumed existing stock. Customer orders consequently became a weaker measure of final demand.

Inference / limited speculation

Trade shows probably helped expand the Dante ecosystem and encouraged OEM and customer adoption. It is possible that strong industry enthusiasm, combined with shortages and long lead times, contributed to customers ordering more inventory than they ultimately required. The available data cannot prove that chain.

The safer interpretation is that trade shows supported long-term demand creation, while the FY25 visibility problem was primarily caused by supply-chain normalisation, shortened ordering horizons, channel inventory and softer end demand.

Hard evidence: need for stronger analytics

Audinate has created a new Data and Insights function that is expected to combine data across Sales, Marketing, Finance, Product and Strategy; establish recurring ELT and Board reporting; support planning and commercial decisions; provide centralised data-science capability; and integrate AI-assisted analytics into the operating model.

Audinate also notes that embedded software licences are delivered on demand, are not generally overstocked and therefore track underlying product consumption more directly than physical chips and modules. This makes software consumption a potentially cleaner demand signal than hardware orders distorted by channel inventory.

Inference: likely analytical response

There is no public statement that the FY25 downgrade directly caused the creation of the Data and Insights role. The timing and responsibilities nevertheless make the connection credible.

A likely analytical requirement is to connect:

  1. trade-show engagement, marketing activity and training;
  2. OEM interest and design wins;
  3. the product-development pipeline and launch timing;
  4. manufacturer and distributor orders;
  5. inventory held across OEM and channel networks;
  6. sell-through, licence consumption, activation and actual usage; and
  7. revenue, gross profit, cash flow and forecast outcomes.

This would allow management to distinguish genuine end-market demand from temporary channel stocking. Real-time demand sensing, integrated inventory tracking, AI-enabled forecasting and scenario modelling are logical elements of that response, but they remain an inference rather than a confirmed management statement.

Conclusion: Trade shows appear to support ecosystem adoption, but the data does not show that they directly drove financial performance or caused the FY25 visibility problem. The inventory correction and shorter order cycle did expose a clear need for more timely demand sensing, channel visibility, scenario planning and cross-functional analytics.

3. Is Audinate making a major shift from hardware to a software or SaaS model?

References: Audinate FY24 Results | Audinate FY25 Results | Audinate 1H26 Results

Hard evidence

The shift is visible in the revenue mix:

MetricFY24FY25
Software revenueUS$15.4mUS$17.1m
Software share of revenueapproximately 25.6%approximately 42.8%
Chips, cards, modules and adaptorsUS$43.8mUS$22.1m
Gross margin74.2%82.1%

Software revenue increased by approximately 11%, while hardware-related revenue declined by approximately 50%. The higher software mix was the principal reason gross margin exceeded 82%.

An important qualification is that part of the apparent mix shift resulted from hardware revenue falling sharply during the inventory correction. It was not solely caused by a sudden acceleration in software revenue.

The strategic product evidence is broader:

  • Dante IP Core and embedded software can replace physical Dante components in some OEM products.
  • Dante Director is Audinate's cloud-based SaaS management product.
  • DVS Pro introduced a subscription model.
  • Iris introduced usage-based subscription revenue.
  • Dante Device Link extends the platform into device control and cloud management.
  • Audinate created a Chief Platform Officer role to integrate audio, video, control, products and services.

In 1H26, embedded software revenue grew 17%, platform software revenue grew 9%, Iris launched as a usage-based subscription and gross margin remained above 82%. These are clear signs of continued software and recurring-revenue expansion.

Hard evidence: important qualification

Audinate explicitly stated that the majority of its revenue remains linked to physical hardware devices installed in real-world venues. It describes the model as an infrastructure and economic-network model rather than a conventional seat-based SaaS model.

Physical Dante-enabled devices remain essential because they create the installed base, interoperability and network effect that generate demand for embedded licences, cloud management, control and related services. Audinate is therefore not abandoning hardware.

Comparison with Cisco

The direction is similar to Cisco's transformation: shifting more value from physical products toward software and subscriptions, increasing recurring revenue, improving gross margins and adding cloud-based management around a large installed hardware base.

The important difference is that Audinate is earlier in the transition and more dependent on OEM-manufactured physical devices. Its likely destination is not a pure SaaS company. A more accurate description is a hybrid AV infrastructure and software-platform company.

The future revenue model is likely to combine:

  • physical adaptors, chips, cards and modules;
  • embedded software licences;
  • OEM royalties or consumption-based licensing;
  • desktop and professional software subscriptions;
  • cloud management subscriptions; and
  • usage-based AI and control services.

Software should reduce some inventory volatility because licences are consumed when required rather than ordered and stored months in advance. It should also generate higher margins, cleaner demand signals and a more predictable recurring-revenue component. FY25 and 1H26 nevertheless show that software is not yet large enough to remove hardware cyclicality or guarantee operating leverage.

Conclusion: Audinate is making a material shift toward software, cloud management and recurring subscriptions, directionally similar to Cisco's software transformation. It is not becoming a pure SaaS company. The more likely model is a high-margin, software-led platform built on a large and still-essential ecosystem of physical Dante-enabled devices.

6. Probing Questions

↑ Back to Top

The job description provides the clearest evidence of the function's intended mandate. The questions below are designed to test how that mandate connects to Audinate's current commercial, operational and data challenges.

Connection to the job description

References: Audinate, Senior Manager, Data and Insights job description
  • The advertisement explicitly calls Data and Insights a new function.
  • The role owns platform choices across warehouse, modelling and visualisation.
  • It must create a recurring ELT and Board reporting cadence.
  • It must establish governance, certified data surfaces and self-service use.
  • It must translate analysis into commercial decisions, not stop at reporting.

1. How does leadership view the current state of Data & Insights at Audinate, particularly the time it takes to turn data into useful insight and the ability of the data to give a clear view of the business? Is the main challenge reliability, speed, inconsistent definitions across business areas, conflicting numbers, or something else?

Why ask: Directly tests why the new function exists and whether there is a genuine data trust, consistency or time-to-insight problem.

Prompted by: The role is explicitly a new central Data & Insights function with responsibility for ELT and Board reporting, governance, self-service data and decision support.

2. Shorter customer lead times and channel inventory rebalancing reduced FY25 visibility and appeared to affect investor confidence. How is Audinate addressing that today? What additional visibility into end demand, customer inventory and channel activity would materially improve forecasting and decision-making?

Why ask: Goes directly to one of the strongest findings in the analysis and creates a natural discussion around real-time demand sensing, inventory visibility, forecasting and AI-enabled analytics.

Prompted by: FY25 visibility weakened as order lead times shortened and OEM/channel inventory rebalanced, making customer orders a less reliable indicator of final demand.

3. How well can Audinate currently connect leading indicators such as trade-show engagement, training activity, OEM design wins and the 12–24 month product-development cycle through to product launches, customer orders, channel inventory, software licence consumption and ultimately end-customer demand and revenue? Where does that chain become difficult to see today?

Why ask: Tests the trade-show hypothesis without assuming causation and gets to the heart of whether Audinate can distinguish interest, pipeline, sell-in, inventory, sell-through and actual consumption.

Prompted by: Trade shows appear to support ecosystem adoption, while design wins can take 12–24 months to translate into repeat manufacturing revenue.

4. As Audinate evolves from a predominantly hardware and embedded-component business toward more software, cloud and recurring revenue, how is that changing the way the business measures performance and forecasts demand? Do you need different KPIs and analytical models for hardware, embedded software, subscriptions and usage-based products?

Why ask: Directly tests the software-transition hypothesis and should reveal whether Audinate is already adapting its commercial metrics and data model to a more complex hybrid platform business.

Prompted by: Software revenue share increased materially in FY25, gross margin rose, and Audinate expanded into cloud management, subscriptions and usage-based products while remaining dependent on the physical Dante ecosystem.

5. If this Data & Insights function is highly successful over the next 12 months, what decisions should leadership be able to make faster or with greater confidence that are difficult today? Where do you see the biggest gaps: data integration, definitions and governance, forecasting, analytical capability, or the way insights are embedded into decision-making?

Why ask: Clarifies the real mandate for the role and converts the analysis into measurable decision outcomes rather than technology delivery.

Prompted by: The job description emphasises decisions enabled, recurring executive reporting, trusted modelled data, self-service analytics and stronger commercial decision support.
