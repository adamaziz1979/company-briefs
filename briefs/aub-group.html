Insurance 101: what is insurance brokerage?

An insurance broker helps people and businesses buy the right insurance. The broker works for the customer, not the insurance company: it finds what could go wrong, shops several insurers, recommends the best cover and helps when a claim is made. "Broking" is the usual Australian word for this business, and it is AUB's core business.

PointIn plain wordsSimple example
InsuranceYou pay a small, certain amount (the premium); the insurer pays a big, uncertain loss (a claim).A café pays $4,000 a year; after a kitchen fire the insurer pays for repairs and lost income.
The brokerA middle person who acts for the customer, like a mortgage broker does for home loans. It never pays claims itself.A plumber with five vans calls one broker instead of ten insurers.
The insurerWrites the policy, carries the risk and pays claims.QBE, Allianz, Zurich, or a Lloyd's syndicate in London.
What a broker doesUnderstand risks, shop the market, recommend, place the cover, help with claims, renew every year.The cheapest quote for a builder excludes flood; the broker spots it and recommends the dearer one.
Who uses brokersMostly businesses, from small to very large; some wealthier individuals.A hair salon needing four policies; a mining company insuring a $500m plant.
How brokers earnCommission from the insurer (often around 10% to 20% of premium for business cover) plus fees.On a $10,000 café policy: $1,500 commission plus a $300 fee.
Underwriting agencyNot a broker: a specialist that writes policies on an insurer's behalf for a niche.An agency that only insures medical clinics, using an insurer's money.

It places about $11b of premium a year for about 1.6m clients and earns mostly commission and fees on it (see Stage 1).
